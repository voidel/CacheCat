FEATURES

Browse and search
your cached textures.

CacheCat reads your viewer’s existing texture cache.
No export or conversion step is required.

01

Newest-first texture grid

Browse a scrolling grid of cached textures, ordered by the viewer’s last-used timestamp. Open an item to see its details and copy its UUID.

02

UUID and semantic search

Try “red silk”, “round gold ornament”, or “weathered stone”. A compact SigLIP model compares your description with the textures on your device. Paste a UUID when you know exactly what you want.

03

Progressive indexing

Indexing happens in the background. A progress bar shows how many embeddings are ready, and searches use them straight away. Partial results are clearly labelled and update as indexing continues.

04

Desktop and browser interfaces

Light and dark modes follow your system preference, with a manual override. The desktop app uses Rust and GPUI; the browser version needs no installation.

LOCAL PROCESSING

Your cache stays
on your device.

CacheCat reads the folder you choose. Texture decoding, embedding generation, and search all happen locally. Your textures, UUIDs, search terms, and embeddings are never uploaded.

REQUIREMENTS AND SUPPORT

Frequently asked questions

Which folder should I open?

Use the cache location shown in your viewer’s settings. You can select that folder or its texturecache subfolder. CacheCat supports the 1.7 and 1.71 texture-cache formats used by Second Life and Firestorm.

Does the browser really process everything locally?

Yes. Network requests download the application, runtime, and public model files. There is no cache-upload endpoint, server-side inference, or analytics in the viewer. Browser storage holds your thumbnails and search index; clearing site data removes them.

What does the first run download?

Browsing and UUID filtering do not need a model. Enabling semantic search downloads roughly 213 MB of quantized SigLIP model assets, plus the browser runtime. Cached assets and unchanged embeddings can be reused on later visits.

Which browser should I use?

Desktop Chrome and Edge provide the richest folder-access experience. Other recent browsers can use folder selection with CPU fallback, subject to their WebAssembly and storage support. GPU acceleration is detected at runtime. Keep the tab open while indexing; browsers can suspend background tabs.

Why are some textures unavailable?

Viewer caches are temporary and may contain partial or evicted textures. CacheCat skips items it cannot decode and reports them separately from pending indexing. Semantic results are similarity-ranked suggestions, not exact matches.
